## Context I brought
Walked Sam through the activation investigation: flat ~58%, two medium-confidence causes (silent runtime field-mapping breakage + onboarding stall), and the ticket digest (24 breakage tickets/quarter, undercounted, resolved only by manual support remapping). Proposed: detection + alerting when a mapping silently breaks.

## Sam's reaction (direct quotes + notes)

**On feasibility of detection:**
- "Detecting it is doable but it's not free. Salesforce doesn't hand you a clean 'a field you care about just changed' event. We'd poll the metadata API, snapshot the schema for each connected org, and diff it against the field mappings we're relying on. When a mapped field's API name, type, or presence changes, we flag it."
- "The tractable version catches the common cases — field rename, type change, a field going missing after a package update. That covers most of what you're describing."
- **Caution on false positives:** "The trap is alert fatigue. A legitimately empty field looks a lot like a broken one. If we cry wolf, admins mute us and we're worse than useless. So v1 has to be conservative about what it calls a break."

**Rough sizing (his estimate, explicitly rough):**
- Detection + a basic alert (email/in-app to the admin) + a simple sync-health indicator: **"about three to four engineer-weeks for a credible v1. Call it one engineer for a month, including not shipping garbage."**
- The guided/auto-remediation piece (Flo re-detects the field and offers to remap): **"that's a different animal — another six to eight weeks at least, and it's got its own failure modes. I would not bundle it into v1."**

**Counter-idea he raised — prevention vs. detection:**
- "Honestly, part of why this breaks is our mapping binds to field labels/names in a brittle way. If we bound to the durable Salesforce field IDs where we can, a rename wouldn't break us at all. I'd rather prevent a class of this than build a smoke detector for it."
- He conceded the limits himself: "Prevention doesn't cover everything — a field genuinely deleted, a type change, a managed-package change can still break you, and it does nothing for the mappings already broken today or for giving reps any visibility. So it's not either/or. Prevention shrinks the problem; detection covers the rest and tells us how often it actually happens."
- Note: **we have no sync-health telemetry today at all.** Sam pointed out that building detection is *also* how we'd finally measure the real breakage rate — "right now you're guessing from tickets; this would give you the actual number."

**On capacity — the real constraint (T1):**
- "Here's my problem. I have four engineers. We're mid-flight on the deeper-Salesforce roadmap, and I've got leadership asking me every other week where we are on Enterprise-tier scoping, plus the AI-summaries thing keeps resurfacing. I cannot do detection *and* Enterprise scoping *and* keep the SFDC depth roadmap on schedule with four people."
- "If you want the detection v1 in this quarter, tell me explicitly what drops. My read: it comes out of either the SFDC-depth work or Enterprise scoping. I'm not going to silently eat it and then get blamed when Enterprise slips."
- **His explicit ask:** "Get me air cover. A real decision that says detection is prioritized over [X] this quarter. Not a hallway 'can you just squeeze it in.'"

## My read (PM, in-meeting)
Sam is not resisting the work — he's resisting absorbing an unfunded mandate. He thinks v1 detection is cheap and worth doing, likes prevention as a complement, and is drawing a hard line that it must come with an explicit deprioritization he can point to. This is exactly the T1 tension made concrete.

## Open items
- Need the explicit tradeoff call: detection v1 vs. what it displaces (SFDC-depth vs. Enterprise scoping).
- Decide v1 scope: detection + alert + sync-health indicator now; guided remediation later; prevention (durable field-ID binding) as a parallel track to size.
- Leadership sign-off on the deprioritization is a T1 item — not Sam's to make.

- **Kind:** interview
- **Date:** 2026-07-03
- **Participants:** PM (interviewer); Nadia Osei — Head of Revenue, Tandem Software (~45 seats, mid-market B2B SaaS). Wears the RevOps hat herself; no dedicated ops or Salesforce staff.
- **Format:** 30-min video call, notes + partial transcript
- **Purpose:** Third independent read on the activation problem — a smaller/earlier-stage account, to test whether the pattern looks the same outside the two enterprise accounts.

---

**PM:** Thanks for the time, Nadia. I'm talking to a range of accounts because our activation numbers are stuck and I want the real picture, not the rosy one. Where does Flo actually stand with your team?

**Nadia:** I'll be honest with you — it never really landed. We signed up, I was excited, and then most of my reps just… never got going on it. I think I have maybe six people out of thirty-something actually using it. The rest bounced off in the first week or two.

**PM:** Tell me about that first week or two. What did "getting going" look like for a rep?

**Nadia:** That's the problem — it looked like a lot. A rep logs in, and there's this setup flow, connect your calendar, connect Salesforce, confirm your mapping, and it's just not obvious what you're supposed to do or why. I'm not technical and neither are most of my reps. I got myself set up because I pushed through it, but I couldn't get the team to. There was no hand-holding, no "here's step one, here's step two, you're done." They'd hit the Salesforce connection step, get confused, and just close the tab and go back to selling.

**PM:** So it's less that something broke, and more that they never finished setting up.

**Nadia:** Right. Nothing broke because nothing ever really started for them. It's not like it worked and then failed — most of them never got to "working" at all. The ones who did get through it are fine, I don't hear complaints from them. It's the getting-there that killed us.

**PM:** You mentioned the Salesforce connection step specifically — was that the sticking point, or was it the whole flow?

**Nadia:** Honestly a bit of both, but the Salesforce part is where I watched two of my reps give up in real time on a screen-share. The field mapping thing — it asks you to match fields and my reps had no idea which Salesforce field was which. That's an admin question and they're not admins. But even before that, the whole thing just needed someone walking them through it, and we didn't have that.

**PM:** That's really useful. Anything else that's shaped how your team feels about it?

**Nadia:** Yeah, and this is the part that stings — for what we're paying, I expected more of my team to be getting value by now. It's not cheap per seat, and I'm looking at a bill where most of the seats aren't even active. My CFO asked me last month why we're paying for thirty-something seats when six people use it. I don't have a great answer.

**PM:** Is the price itself the issue, or —

**Nadia:** I mean… if it were free I wouldn't be complaining, right? But no, if I'm honest, if all thirty reps were using it and closing more deals, I wouldn't blink at the price. The price only feels bad because the value isn't there yet. It's not that it's overpriced in a vacuum, it's that I'm paying for activation I'm not getting. Fix the "my team actually uses it" problem and the price conversation goes away.

**PM:** That's a really clear way to put it. So if we made that first-run experience genuinely guided — a rep could get to a working sync without needing you or an admin —

**Nadia:** That would change everything for us. That's the whole game for a team like mine. I don't need more features. I need my reps to get from signup to "it's working" without me holding their hand one by one. If they get there, they stay, and the price stops being a conversation.

**PM:** Understood. This is exactly the perspective I was missing. Thank you, Nadia.

**Nadia:** Happy to help. Just make it easier to start.
